Tim Berners-Lee

Sir Tim Berners-Lee is a British computer scientist who invented the World Wide Web in 1989 while working at CERN, the European physics laboratory near Geneva. He wrote the first web browser and web server, and he created the core technologies that still power the web today: HTML, URLs, and HTTP. In 1994 he founded the World Wide Web Consortium (W3C) to guide the web's long-term development, and he has been a strong advocate for keeping it open and free for everyone.
